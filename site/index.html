    Price cuts can be ranked two ways. "Cut in dollars" ranks by the amount, which favours dear cars; "cut as a share of the price" ranks by the fraction taken off, so $1,000 off a $5,000 car ranks above $1,500 off a $30,000 one.

    Best value compares a car's price with what similar cars in this list ask: the same model within two years and 30,000 miles, adjusted for year, mileage, trim and engine. A car is marked only when it sits a tenth or more under that, and only when there are at least three similar cars to go by. The estimate cannot see condition, accident history or a branded title, and it is loosest for cars under $10,000, so treat a mark as a reason to look, not as an appraisal.

    Each price includes the dealer's own fee wherever its site states one, because the buyer pays it either way: some dealers advertise the price with the fee in it and some show the fee apart, so a price here can be higher than the one on a dealer's page by exactly that fee. Where no fee is stated, expect the dealer to add one. The figure "with tax" adds Tennessee sales tax for a Memphis buyer: 7% of the price, plus 2.75% of the first $1,600, plus 2.75% of the next $1,600. Title and registration are extra. "One owner" and "Carfax: good value" repeat the badge Carfax shows on the dealer's listing; the Carfax link opens the free report that dealer provides, and the recall check opens the government's lookup for that exact car.

    Cars marked "private sale" are Craigslist's for-sale-by-owner listings from Memphis and the Craigslist cities around it, some of them two hours' drive away: "Within (miles)" keeps the ones near enough, measured in a straight line from downtown Memphis to the seller's map pin or the dealer's lot. A car whose distance is not known, such as one Carvana delivers, is never hidden by it. Their year, mileage and title are what the seller typed, and nobody has checked them; few give a VIN. They are set against what dealers ask for similar cars, and a private seller usually asks less than a dealer does, so most of them show as under. Read the mark as "cheaper than a lot would be": the further under, the more it is worth a look, and the more it is worth asking why. A car whose seller says the title is rebuilt, salvage or otherwise not clean is shown without that comparison.

    Prices are asking prices read from each dealer's own website or the seller's own listing. A price cut compares today's price with the first price this tracker saw for that car, so a car cut before tracking began shows no cut.

    Days listed counts from the dealer's own stock date or the day the seller posted, where given, otherwise from the first day the tracker saw the car. Dealer fees, tax and title are not included.
