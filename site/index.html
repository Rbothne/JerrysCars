    Price cuts can be ranked two ways. "Cut in dollars" ranks by the amount, which favours dear cars; "cut as a share of the price" ranks by the fraction taken off, so $1,000 off a $5,000 car ranks above $1,500 off a $30,000 one.

    Each car is set beside what cars like it ask: the same model within two years and 30,000 miles, moved to this car's year and mileage, checked against a price model of the whole list. Where fewer than three are that close, the search widens by steps to six years and 90,000 miles and the card says how far it went. Where there are none at all, the card gives a rough figure from the model alone. The range is where six in ten asking prices fell around such a figure. A car is marked "under similar cars" only when it asks well under: a tenth for a dealer's car with close matches, 15% with wider ones, 30% for a private seller's. A rough figure never earns a mark. No figure can see condition, accident history or what is wrong with the car, so treat a mark as a reason to look, not as an appraisal.

    Each price includes the dealer's own fee wherever its site states one, because the buyer pays it either way: some dealers advertise the price with the fee in it and some show the fee apart, so a price here can be higher than the one on a dealer's page by exactly that fee. Where no fee is stated, expect the dealer to add one. The figure "with tax" adds Tennessee sales tax for a Memphis buyer: 7% of the price, plus 2.75% of the first $1,600, plus 2.75% of the next $1,600. Title and registration are extra. "One owner" and "Carfax: good value" repeat the badge Carfax shows on the dealer's listing; the Carfax link opens the free report that dealer provides, and the recall check opens the government's lookup for that exact car.

    Cars marked "private sale" are Craigslist's for-sale-by-owner listings from Memphis and the Craigslist cities around it, some of them two hours' drive away: "Within (miles)" keeps the ones near enough, measured in a straight line from downtown Memphis to the seller's map pin or the dealer's lot. A car whose distance is not known, such as one Carvana delivers, is never hidden by it. Their year, mileage and title are what the seller typed, and nobody has checked them; few give a VIN. A private seller asks less than a dealer does for the same car, and the older the car the wider that gap, so a private car is set beside what private sellers ask. Those figures are looser than a dealer's: two private sellers of the same model often differ by a third. A car whose seller says the title is rebuilt, salvage or has a lien is shown beside what a clean one asks and is never marked; one for parts or without a title gets no figure.

    Prices are asking prices read from each dealer's own website or the seller's own listing. A price cut compares today's price with the first price this tracker saw for that car, so a car cut before tracking began shows no cut.

    Days listed counts from the dealer's own stock date or the day the seller posted, where given, otherwise from the first day the tracker saw the car. Dealer fees, tax and title are not included.
